Verid Consortium

The cloistered Verid built a faith around the one gate in known space that still runs both ways.

The Verid are a faith. Not the shouting kind — the kind that took its entire civilisation offshore of the galaxy’s noise, into a closed cluster of systems nobody else can reach, and let known space grow up without it.

Every religion in known space is built on something. The Teralith have their monolith and licence the network in its name. The Verid have a door.

Behaviour

Verid vessels are rare in the core systems and unmistakable when they appear: smooth, pale hulls, no visible weapons, no transponder signal that resolves to a known faction. They observe. They record. They do not engage unless engaged, and when they do engage, they are devastating in ways that suggest centuries of preparation.

They trade in readings. Not goods, not credits — interpretation. Bring a Verid something old and they will tell you what it means, at length, with total serenity, and their answer will be the answer their scripture already had. Their buyers have argued for generations over whether that is worth the price. It is never priced in money.

Relationship to the Ancients

The network is a galaxy of one-way roads. A crossing needs a live sender at one end and a live receiver at the other, and after all this time most pairs are missing a half — you go somewhere and you find your own way home. The Verid found a lane that never lost anything. Both gates whole, both directions lit, still holding the road open with nobody tending it.

They found it first, and they have held it ever since. Their cluster hangs off the far side of it, which is why they can be anywhere and no one can follow them home.

From that they take their whole claim: the gate did not fail for them, so they were meant to have it. Kinship, they call it — not owners of the road but relatives of the road-builders, keeping a thing that was always family property. Nobody outside the order can check a word of it. Nobody outside the order has ever been allowed near enough to try, and the Verid have made a discipline out of keeping it that way. What they actually have is a working gate and a very old book about it.

In the field

Verid ships will not engage first. If they are present in a system, they are watching something, and whatever it is matters enough to justify the crossing. Do not read neutrality as safety. The Verid’s definition of an acceptable outcome has never been shared with anyone else.

Scanners return nothing useful on a Verid hull — no cargo read, no battle profile, sometimes not even a name. That is not a scanner limitation. It is a choice they made about you, renewed every time you look. Provoke one and the silence ends all at once.

What a Verid will trade for

Not credits, and not cargo. The stories all agree on the shape of it: they take things that cost you. A heading you earned the hard way. Something you dug out of a ruin and are still carrying. A crystal you could have burned on a jump.

Offer nothing that cost anything and the channel simply closes. Offer something real and you get a reading — a place, a name, a line of their scripture aimed at your particular question — delivered as settled fact by people who have never once said we think. Pilots who have paid come away certain they were told something. Very few of them agree afterwards on what.
