Kithar

Sealed-suit Kithar brokers trade secrets from scarab ships no one has ever seen opened — or disabled.

No one has ever seen a Kithar.

You have seen the suit: a sealed encounter shell, smooth and unhurried, present at every dock where information changes hands. Whatever is inside does not come out. Not for trade, not for medicine, not for death — there is no confirmed record of a Kithar suit being opened, anywhere, by anyone. The favourite story says they come from the crushing deep layers of a gas giant in some neutron-star system, and that everywhere else in the galaxy is a vacuum to them — the suit not a custom but a necessity. It is a tidy story. Nobody has confirmed a word of it, and the Kithar, who sell every other secret in known space, do not sell this one.

Behaviour

The Kithar have no homeworld, no territory, and no navy. A diffuse network of information brokers, logistics agents, and silent partners, they operate in the spaces between the established powers, and the network itself is the only nation they answer to. They do not manufacture goods. They manufacture knowledge. A Kithar broker can tell you where the Korrathi will raid next week, what the Teralith are paying for gate calibration parts, and exactly how much Blackwater knows about your cargo — assuming you have something to trade in return.

They are not hostile. They are not friendly. They are transactional, in a way that makes the Teralith look sentimental.

Relationship to the Ancients

The Kithar network predates every successor civilisation except Blackwater — and the two have an unspoken arrangement: Blackwater owns the logs, the Kithar own the routes. No Kithar agent has ever confirmed whether their network extends into Ancient-era records. The assumption is that it does, and that the price for that data is beyond anything any faction can pay.

In the field

The Kithar field two hulls and no others: a small shuttle and a long transport, both the encounter suit at scale. Sealed gold-green scarab shells, no windows, no visible engines, no seam anyone has ever found — one pale gold sensor eye watching from the head, and nothing else on the hull at all. A Kithar ship at rest and a Kithar ship underway look identical.

Nobody attacks a Kithar ship. This is not a treaty and it is not fear of reprisal — it is bookkeeping. In all the recorded history of every faction that keeps records, no Kithar hull has ever been disabled, boarded, salvaged, or recovered. Raiders who fired on one tell contradictory stories about what happened next, and none of the stories end with a wreck. At some point every power in known space did the same arithmetic: the attempt has no confirmed payoff and a perfect record of failure, and the Kithar remember who tried. Ask a broker how their ships manage it and you will get terms. Asking, after all, is already a negotiation.

Where they stand

A Kithar hull reads neutral on your nameplate, and so does every other hull’s read on theirs. That is the whole point of them. They are on mildly good terms with you, with the haulers, and with the independents, for the plain reason that all three are people a broker might one day sell to.

The instructive part is the raiders. Pirates are hostile to everything that isn’t a pirate — everything except this. A Kessler wing will not cross the line into open hostility with a scarab shell, because the arithmetic that stopped every other power stopped them too. It is the only place in known space where the raiders’ hostility simply stops.
