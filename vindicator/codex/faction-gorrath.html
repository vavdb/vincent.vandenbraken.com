Gorrath

The numerous Gorrath sweep outward, stripping resources before moving to new territory.

Fecund. Fast. Short-lived and numerous. The Gorrath are a reptilian species whose reproductive cycle outpaces their own industrial capacity — they produce more bodies than they can feed, which means they are always, structurally, in need of more territory.

A Gorrath warship is crewed by three generations, two of which will die before the ship returns to port. They accept this. Their culture is built around the certainty of a short life: they fight young, breed early, and die without fear.

Their ships say it plainly. Every Gorrath hull wears the same overlapping scale-lap plating, every plate pressed from the same die, every hull identical to the one beside it — cheap stamped metal, die marks still showing, built faster than it can be destroyed. Nothing is bespoke because nothing is expected to last. Their capitals are not larger sculpted ships at all; they are rafts, the same stamped segment repeated again and again around a spine, a fleet’s worth of press-forge output flying in one piece.

Behaviour

Gorrath fleets push outward. They do not hold ground the way the Teralith do — they sweep, claim, exhaust, and move on. A system that survives a Gorrath passage is usually stripped of accessible resources but otherwise intact; they lack the infrastructure for deep mining. They are locusts, not settlers.

They respect strength. A faction that fights the Gorrath to a standstill earns their grudging respect — and, more practically, a generation of peace while the survivors rebuild and the next wave matures.

See a Gorrath brood city once and their doctrine stops being a mystery. Terraced ranks of identical scale-roofed halls climb a mound, and beneath every hall sit the feed pens — dark, churning, always full. The city is one organism being fed, and the fleets are what it exhales. Territory is not conquest to the Gorrath. It is metabolism.

Relationship to the Ancients

The Gorrath are the youngest successor species. They have no myths about the Ancients. They have trophies — Ancient components salvaged from dead gates, repurposed as clan symbols, weapon inlays, and religious icons. They do not understand what they hold. They only know it is old and strong.

In the field

A Gorrath encounter is a pressure event. They hit hard, replace losses fast, and do not negotiate mid-battle. The spearpoint is the cannon-dart: a small stamped seed-shape with a gun, thrown at you in numbers. Every fleet ledger in known space files them as drones, because no one builds something that expendable and puts a pilot in it. The Gorrath do. Every dart carries a canopy slit and a young crew flying their first and likely last commission, and they fly it without fear, because fear is for species with time. The best defence is to make the engagement expensive enough that the next wave looks for easier prey.

Where they stand

A Gorrath hull reads neutral to you on the nameplate, which says less than you would like. They have no quarrel with you on record and no fondness either; nobody outside the broods can say with confidence what earns a Gorrath’s hostility, and the ones who found out are not filing reports. What is certain is that they shoot raiders — a brood sweeping a system does not share it — so a Kessler wing is one thing you will never see flying beside an ochre scale-lap hull.
